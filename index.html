    💡 Why this is here

    This map shows exactly which stars and constellations are above you at the place and time you chose. Recognising constellations is the first skill every stargazer needs: once you know them, you can use them to find your direction, find planets and plan what to look at.

    👆 How to use it
    1. Read the map as the sky above you: the outer circle is the horizon and the centre is straight above your head. The letters N, E, S, W around the edge are the directions.
    2. Outside, face the direction you want to look and turn the map so that direction’s letter is at the bottom. The bottom half of the map then matches the sky in front of you.
    3. Click a constellation (or a name button under the map) to read what it is, where to look and how it helps you navigate.
    4. Drag the sky in a circle or press ▶ Play night to watch the stars move during the night.
    5. Press 🎯 Quiz mode to test yourself: click the constellation named under the map.

    02

    Celestial Navigation

    Learn to find north, south, east and west using only the stars. No prior knowledge needed.

    💡 Why this is here

    Before GPS, people crossing the desert or sailing the Gulf found their way using the stars. These skills still work today when your phone has no signal or battery. They also explain how the sky works: why stars move, why one star stays still, and why the sky looks different depending on where you are.

    👆 How to use it
    1. Choose a mission tab. Start with Mission 1: each mission builds on the one before.
    2. Read the short introduction. If you see a word you do not know, tap it under “Key words” to read what it means.
    3. Press Next step. Each step adds one thing to the picture on the left, so follow along with your eyes.
    4. At the last step, answer the Check yourself question. A ✓ appears on the tab when you get it right.

    03

    Celestial Coordinates

    Learn how astronomers describe exactly where a star is in the sky.

    💡 Why this is here

    To find a star you need to know where to look. Instructions like “look 30° up in the south-east” and every star app use the words on these cards. Altitude and azimuth tell you where to look from where you stand. Right ascension and declination are a fixed address for each star, used in star charts and catalogues.

    👆 How to use it
    1. Tap a card to flip it and read what the word means. Tap it again to flip it back.
    2. In the dome on the right, drag the star with your mouse or finger.
    3. Watch the two numbers change. Altitude is how high the star is (0° = on the horizon, 90° = straight up). Azimuth is its compass direction (0° = north, 90° = east, 180° = south, 270° = west).
    4. Challenge: move the star to 45° altitude in the south-east (135°).

    04

    Light Pollution & Conditions

    See how city lights hide the stars, and what else affects how much you can see.

    💡 Why this is here

    Light from streets and buildings makes the sky glow and hides faint stars. Most people in the UAE live in bright coastal cities, so moving to a dark place is the biggest single thing you can do to see more stars. Astronomers measure sky darkness with the Bortle scale, from 1 (darkest desert) to 9 (city centre). Weather and the Moon matter too.

    👆 How to use it
    1. Drag the slider to the left, from the city (9) towards the desert (1).
    2. Watch the picture: more stars appear, and the Milky Way shows up once the sky is dark enough.
    3. Read the box under the slider: what the sky looks like at that level and where in the UAE you would find it.
    4. Tap the five buttons below to learn about other things that affect what you see.

    05

    The UAE Sky Calendar

    Find out what you can see each month, and when the weather is best.

    💡 Why this is here

    The sky changes during the year because Earth travels around the Sun, so at night we face a different part of space each month. UAE weather changes a lot too: cool, clear winters are the best season, and hot, humid summers are the hardest. Knowing both helps you choose the best night to go out.

    👆 How to use it
    1. Tap a month on the wheel. The current month is selected when the page opens.
    2. Read which constellations are up in the evening, any special event such as a meteor shower, and the usual weather.
    3. The stars in the middle of the wheel rate the month for stargazing: ★★★★★ = best.
    4. Press See this sky in the Sky Explorer to open the star map for that month.

    06

    Tools & Technology

    Free and paid apps and websites that help you find stars and plan a night out.

    💡 Why this is here

    Modern astronomy relies on technology. Apps use your phone’s GPS and motion sensors to label the stars you point at. Websites forecast clear nights and map dark places. Tools like these let a beginner do in minutes what used to take years of practice, which is the scientific and technical innovation behind this project.

    👆 How to use it
    1. Read each card to see what the tool does and what it is best for.
    2. To start, try one planning tool (Stellarium) and one weather tool (Clear Outside).
    3. Outside, switch on the app’s red or night mode if it has one, and turn your screen brightness down. Bright screens stop your eyes adjusting to the dark.

    About This Project

    🎯 Learning goal

    To deepen my knowledge of celestial navigation and constellation mapping across the UAE.

    🌍 Global context

    Scientific and technical innovation. This website uses digital tools to make scientific knowledge about the night sky easy to explore for anyone, even with no background in astronomy.

    ✨ Why the UAE?

    Star visibility in the UAE changes a lot with artificial light, buildings, location, humidity and dust. Comparing sites shows how the environment shapes what we can see.
